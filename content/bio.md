Before beginning my doctoral studies, I received an M.A. in Global Studies from The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen, and a B.M. in Public Affairs Administration from Northwest A&F University.

## Questions I Care About

I often wonder what becomes of a policy once it leaves the page and enters everyday life. During fieldwork in rural communities, I saw that similar policies could leave very different traces in different places. People understand them through their own experiences and respond from within existing relationships, resources, and ways of life. This is where institutions, technologies, and local communities meet—and reality is often more circuitous, and more revealing, than plans on paper.

I have therefore become interested not only in whether a policy works, but also in how an outcome gradually takes shape through many small interactions: how people interpret rules, how rules alter choices, and how technology rearranges the relationship between people and institutions. Quantitative analysis and computational methods offer ways of observing these processes, but I also want to remain attentive to context. Data can reveal patterns, but they cannot replace an understanding of how people live, act, and make choices.

I am currently especially interested in pursuing two related directions. First, I hope to use agent-based modeling to conduct social simulations and examine how individual behavior, interactions among actors, and institutional arrangements jointly shape governance and policy implementation. By comparing different governance rules and policy scenarios, I hope to better understand the dynamics of complex social systems, test the scope of existing theoretical explanations, and explore how they might be extended. Second, I am interested in the changes that occur when artificial intelligence becomes embedded in government digital platforms and administrative systems, including how it affects administrative decision-making, public services, and interactions between governments and citizens, as well as the responsibilities and risks that follow.

My research direction continues to evolve. What draws me most is the possibility of bringing rigorous empirical analysis and computational modeling into conversation with a concern for people, places, and institutions. To me, research is not only about offering answers; it is also about learning to observe more patiently, ask more precise questions, and approach a complex world with greater humility.
