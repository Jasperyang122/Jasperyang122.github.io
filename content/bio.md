I am an incoming Ph.D. student in Management Science and Engineering at the School of Management, Harbin Institute of Technology, under the supervision of [Prof. Yupan Zhao](https://homepage.hit.edu.cn/zhaoyupan).

Before beginning my doctoral studies, I received an M.A. in Global Studies from The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen, and a B.M. in Public Affairs Administration from Northwest A&F University. My research interests lie at the intersection of computational social science, digital governance, public policy, and rural and regional development.

## Finding My Way into Research

My interest in research did not begin with a clear academic plan. It grew gradually from fieldwork, social practice, and a desire to understand why well-designed policies can produce very different outcomes in different places.

During my undergraduate studies, I participated in field investigations in rural communities and worked with survey data concerning rural industries, digital governance, and farmers’ economic participation. These experiences introduced me to the distance that can sometimes exist between policy intentions and people’s everyday realities. I became increasingly interested in how institutions, technologies, and local conditions jointly shape individual choices and regional development.

That curiosity led me toward quantitative research. I began learning econometrics, spatial analysis, machine learning, and policy-text analysis, not simply as technical tools, but as different ways of asking better questions. My subsequent research has examined digital rural development, agricultural and rural modernization, migration and settlement, and the social consequences of regional transformation.

As a research assistant, I have worked with government documents, statistical yearbooks, regional panel data, and online platform materials. I have also participated in academic writing and policy research, including a policy consultation report adopted by the People’s Government of Shaanxi Province. These experiences have strengthened my belief that research should remain connected to real social problems.
